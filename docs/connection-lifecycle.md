# Connection lifecycle

A connection should be treated as a lifecycle rather than a one-time event.

## 1. Prepare

Clarify the purpose, participants, constraints, risks, and success measures. Assign an accountable owner and an independent point of contact for concerns where possible.

## 2. Match

Use transparent criteria. Avoid revealing sensitive participant information during matching. Give participants a meaningful opportunity to accept, decline, or request a different match.

## 3. Establish

Set expectations for communication frequency, acceptable topics, boundaries, confidentiality, and support. Provide an easy way to ask for help.

## 4. Maintain

Check that the connection remains wanted, safe, and useful. Review changes in circumstances and avoid creating pressure to continue.

## 5. Pause or end

A participant may pause or end a connection without punishment. Explain what happens to shared information, access, and outstanding commitments. When appropriate, offer a safe transition or referral.

## 6. Learn

Evaluate outcomes using the least identifying data possible. Include participant feedback, near misses, access barriers, and unintended effects. Publish only appropriately aggregated findings.

## Suggested measures

- participant-reported sense of safety and agency;
- response and retention rates, interpreted cautiously;
- accessibility and support requests;
- concerns raised and resolution time; and
- reasons for pausing or ending participation.
