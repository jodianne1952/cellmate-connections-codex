# Safety and privacy

This document is a baseline, not legal advice. Confirm requirements with qualified local experts and the relevant institution or authority before deployment.

## Threat model

For each connection, ask:

- Who could be harmed by disclosure, impersonation, coercion, or unwanted contact?
- What information could identify or locate a participant?
- What happens if a device, account, message, or record is compromised?
- Which participants have less power or fewer safe alternatives?
- What emergency or safeguarding situations require escalation?

## Minimum safeguards

- Collect only necessary information.
- Use role-based access and remove access promptly.
- Prefer secure, approved communication channels.
- Avoid putting identifying details in public repositories or shared documents.
- Set retention and deletion rules before collecting data.
- Keep an auditable record of material decisions without storing unnecessary personal details.
- Provide a plain-language incident reporting route.
- Test procedures with people who have relevant lived experience.

## Boundaries

Do not promise absolute confidentiality when safety, law, or policy may require disclosure. Explain limits before participation begins. Facilitators should not provide professional legal, medical, or mental-health services unless qualified and authorized to do so.

## Incident response

1. Protect immediate safety.
2. Limit further access or disclosure.
3. Notify the designated response contact.
4. Record facts, actions, and times—without speculation.
5. Communicate honestly with affected people when safe and appropriate.
6. Review the cause and update safeguards.
